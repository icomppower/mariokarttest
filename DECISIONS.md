# DECISIONS

## Why the centerline is a mesh, not a glTF animation path

glTF has no first-class curve type. The Blender exporter can turn a curve
into a mesh, or bake it into an animation channel (keyframed translation of
an empty). We export it as an **edges-only mesh** because:

- The sim needs the polyline directly: arc length, closest-point projection,
  tangents. A mesh gives those as raw vertex/edge arrays with no decoding.
- Animation channels carry time, not arc length; the sampling rate would
  couple to the exporter's keyframe settings, and the sim would have to
  re-derive the loop and its winding from timestamps.
- A mesh survives every viewer and every glTF tool; an animation-only path
  gets dropped by anything that strips animations, including our own export
  flags (`export_animations=False`).
- Vertex order in an exported mesh is not guaranteed, so the loader walks the
  edge loop from the vertex nearest `TRACK_START` and follows the start
  heading. That makes the contract "one closed loop" rather than "N ordered
  vertices", which is also what an artist naturally produces.

## Why on-track is a raycast, not a distance-from-spline check

A distance-from-spline test assumes the track is a constant-width ribbon.
Anything an artist adds in Blender — a widened braking zone, a chicane
kerb, a shortcut plate, a hairpin with an inner runoff — silently breaks it.
Raycasting against `TRACK_SURFACE` means **whatever is drivable in Blender is
drivable in the game**, with no second description of the track to keep in
sync. It also gives the surface height for free (hills, bridges), and the
sim measures track width *from* the raycast (`Track.widthAt`), so the AI
adapts to edits too. The cost is a tiny triangle grid; the sim raycasts
~12 times per kart per tick, well under a millisecond.

## Why assets are generated by a `bpy` script rather than hand-modeled

This session is headless: there is no Blender UI, only `import bpy`. The
script is the only way to author here, and it has upsides that survive
after Johnny takes over in the UI:

- Every asset has a reproducible origin. `python tools/build_assets.py`
  regenerates the same `.blend`s and `.glb`s, so the pipeline itself is
  tested, not just its output.
- The contract (object names, forward axis, applied transforms, `extras`)
  is enforced in code at export time, so a hand edit that breaks it fails
  with a named error instead of a silent gameplay bug (G3).
- The editable `.blend` is still the source of truth once it exists.
  `--from-blend` skips generation and only validates + exports, so hand
  edits are never overwritten by the generator.

## Why a continuous distance counter for laps

The prototype-style "checkpoint then start line" approach breaks for karts
that spawn behind the line (their first crossing counts) and needs special
cases for reversing. Accumulating signed distance along the centerline
makes lap = floor(dist / length): spawning behind the line is negative
distance, reversing over the line naturally un-counts, and ranking is the
same number. Teleport-sized jumps (>25 m/tick) are ignored so projection
glitches cannot award progress.
